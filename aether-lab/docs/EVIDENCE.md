Critic scores are an AI judgement (Muse Spark on renders, 0-10), not a measurement. The gates and IoU are computed by code. From this benchmark on, IoU uses ONE scale for every view (px per mm from the side view's length; the model is only centred, never stretched), so proportions count.

**Targets** (engineering acceptance targets, not results already reached): valid solids 100 %; component list completeness >= 95 %; silhouette IoU >= 0.95 per view on four-view drawings (one scale); floating parts and unintended overlaps 0; critic >= 9 / 10 with no critical defect. A single-view drawing cannot fix hidden geometry, so there the target is honest labelling of what is inferred, not a number.

**Why lower than the car sheet above (a known miss, not tuned away):** this car is an open roadster. The vehicle module (`cad/vehicle.py`) assumes a closed body, so it covers the open cockpit with a roof-like skin (built, not drawn) and builds neither the windscreen nor the roll bar (drawn, not built). That costs most of the side IoU and, with the windscreen frame, most of the end views' IoU (`output/cad/cobra/reproject_*.png`). Open-top bodies need their own cockpit model.

## Limits (said plainly)

- The drawings carry no dimension: real sizes are assumed and labelled until the user gives one.
- The car sheet of sections 1, 2, 6 and 7 is a third-party drawing, used for measurement and not redistributed; the CC0 sheet of section 1b is the public input.
- Historical: the earlier rule-based CAD pipeline was implemented for vehicles only (cad/vehicle.py); the current AI-written CAD has no object-specific code.
- Silhouette agreement is necessary, not sufficient: cross-sections between the views are inferred.
- A drawn car body is one solid (no cabin): interior parts sit inside the solid, shown by the section view.
- All optics are simulations; no holographic display has been built.
