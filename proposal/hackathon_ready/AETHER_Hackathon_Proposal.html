1. Summary

What AETHER is. AETHER’s long-term goal is a bank-card-sized device (85.6 × 54 mm) that puts a near-human-size true hologram into the room. Everyone sees it without a headset, can touch it with a finger, and nothing is sprayed into the air. It is the Iron Man scene, done honestly.

Why nobody has built it. Most products sold as “holograms” are reflections, light-field screens or fog. We started from the wave equation and proved one key fact: with optics alone, a true hologram is always a window (Window Law H1). We tried two workarounds nobody has applied this way before and calculated both to concrete numbers. Both are closed. That leaves one door open: make the air at the image point emit light by itself. Today this needs plasma-level intensity (about 10¹³ W/cm²). Our screening shows the number of air molecules and the total light needed are not the bottleneck. The bottleneck is a molecular pathway nobody has found yet.

Strategy: two tracks.

Track What Time Risk
Product (buildable now) Phase 0 AETHER Window software → Phase 1 window-scale true-hologram card 0–36 months Engineering, manageable
Research (the big prize) Find a new mechanism that makes air glow locally under weak light 2–10 years High scientific risk, but it would create a new product category

What we build at this hackathon. AETHER Window, the Phase 0 software. It turns any laptop and webcam into a head-tracked 3D window you control with your hands and voice, with Meta’s models as the brain and an interactive explanation of the physics on top. It costs nothing to run.

What already exists:

  • A first-principles physics theory: Window Law H1, Scattering-Focus Law H3, superoscillation analysis, and an air-emission screening.
  • A working AETHER Window prototype on Windows (double-click start.bat).
  • A full technology and competitor survey.

2. The problem

2.1 What “true holography” means

A true hologram rebuilds an object’s entire light wave by interference and diffraction. So:

  1. your eyes focus on it as on a real object;
  2. moving your head shows you its side;
  3. no glasses are needed.

2.2 What is sold as “holograms” today

Name How it works Problem
Pepper’s ghost, holo film Angled glass or film reflects a screen Flat image, needs glass
Light-field screens (Looking Glass, Sony) Microlenses send different images in different directions Simulated 3D; eyes cannot truly focus; image stays inside the screen
Fog screens, mist columns Projection onto water mist Puts matter into the air
Spinning mirrors or screens A fast rotating surface sweeps a volume Large mechanical rig
Laser plasma Intense pulses ionise air into dots of light Noise, ozone, eye hazard, expensive

3. What we derived from first principles

We started from three facts only: light obeys the linear wave equation in air, the eye senses only the wave entering its pupil, and energy is conserved. Full derivations are in AETHER Physics Theory v3 (theory folder).

Result What it says Numbers
H1 Window Law By the Kirchhoff–Helmholtz integral, all light in the room comes from the device. You see image point P only if the line from your eye through P continues on and hits the card. A true hologram is a window. A card at 40 cm spans 12.2° × 7.7°. A 3.7 µm phase chip gives ±4.1°; 0.5 µm would give ±32°. The card can hold at most ~6.6 × 10¹⁰ pixels.
Workaround 1: superoscillation A band-limited wave can locally fake a direction it does not contain, so light could seem to come from outside the window. ~2,260 extra phase cycles across a 4 mm pupil, energy cost ~e⁻²⁰⁰⁰. Closed.
Workaround 2: the room as the hologram (H3, new) Shape the laser so light scattered by the walls converges in mid-room. Geometrically this beats H1. We derived that focusing efficiency is at most N / N_total. 60 m² of wall gives N_total ≈ 8.5 × 10¹⁴. Even at the card’s theoretical limit, efficiency is ~0.008%, so a human-size image needs kilowatts of laser. Closed.
The one open door H1 and H3 hold for every linear, passive medium. The only exit is for the air at P to create new light. Today: 10¹³–10¹⁴ W/cm² (plasma).

What our screening found. A human-size image needs about 113 lumens. That means each air molecule on the image surface only has to emit once every ~18 hours. A two-photon estimate puts the needed peak intensity ~50,000× below the plasma threshold. Molecule count and intensity are not the bottleneck. The missing piece is a nitrogen or oxygen energy pathway that weak light can excite and that emits visible light. The best lead is the red glow of the aurora (the N₂ first-positive band), which near-infrared light can recycle so one molecule emits many times.

Discovery target: make a chosen point in air emit visible light (400–700 nm) at peak intensity ≤ 10⁸–10⁹ W/cm², eye-safe, with ≤ 100 W total for a human-size image.

H3 is a new result in its own right and can be published.

Phase Name What Time (est.) Gate
0 AETHER Window Software: head-tracked 3D window, gestures, AI voice, any laptop Now–3 months Tracking accuracy on real users
1a Holography dev kit Desktop true hologram: off-the-shelf phase chip + lasers + eye tracking 6–12 months Floating image that eyes can focus on
1b AETHER Holo Card Bank-card-size window-scale true hologram 18–36 months Thickness, power, cost
2 Air-emission research Computational screening → university lab partners Parallel, 2–10 years Emission threshold lowered ≥ 10⁴×
3 Room-scale AETHER Starts only if Phase 2 succeeds — —

Phase 1 in one paragraph. The card holds tiny RGB lasers and a phase-modulator chip that displays a computed hologram, rebuilding the object’s light wave in the air in front of the card. An eye-tracking camera finds the viewer’s pupils and steers the viewing window onto them every frame (the SeeReal approach), which cuts the pixels needed by orders of magnitude. The image is a true hologram that floats above the card and can be touched, viewed through the card as H1 requires. Estimated bill of materials at volume: US$170–365; estimated retail US$499–899.

5. What we build during the hackathon

AETHER Window needs nothing but a laptop and its webcam. It validates the interaction model that the Holo Card will use, and it teaches the physics.

Feature Description Status
Screen becomes a window The webcam tracks the irises and estimates the eye’s 3D position. Off-axis (generalised perspective) projection makes objects float up to ~4 cm in front of the glass and stay fixed in space as you move. Working
Hands Pinch to grab and rotate, two hands apart to explode a model, point to highlight. Mouse mode (M) as a fallback. Working
Window Law visualiser Press W: a card-sized window appears on screen with live numbers and a top-down light-cone view. Move your head and the object vanishes exactly when your line of sight leaves the card, so judges see H1. Working
Voice and AI on Meta models Speech such as “take the engine apart” or “what does the piston do?” becomes structured scene actions and spoken explanations via the Meta Model API (Llama). Model adapter ready (one config line); on-device keyword fallback works today; Llama connected during event
Real objects into 3D Press P: an object held up to the camera is cut out and turned into a layered 3D card inside the window. Pipeline working with a local cut-out; SAM 3.1 plugs into the server during event
Two viewers Press T: the screen splits into two windows, each rendered for one tracked person’s eyes. Working
English / Chinese Press L to switch UI, speech recognition and voice. Working

Demo scenes: engine, solar system, DNA.

Technical stack: Three.js, MediaPipe Face and Hand Landmarker, One-Euro filtering, a local Node server with zero install on Windows, and a structured action layer between the language model and the 3D scene (JSON actions with an offline fallback).

6. How Meta’s models are used

  1. Llama via the Meta Model API is the scene brain. It turns free speech into a fixed action schema (explode, rotate, highlight part, switch scene, explain) and writes short spoken explanations grounded in the scene’s part list.
  2. SAM 3.1 turns real objects from the webcam into segmented assets for the 3D window.
  3. Physics tutor. In Window Law mode, Llama answers “why did it disappear?” using the live numbers from the visualiser, so the demo explains itself.

7. Three-minute demo

  1. Move your head: the engine stays fixed in space behind the “window”.
  2. Say “take it apart”, then pinch and rotate a piston.
  3. Hold up a real object: SAM 3.1 turns it into a 3D model.
  4. Press W: “Every hologram is a window. Here is why, and here is the one experiment that could change it.”

9. Honesty and risks

  • The room-scale hologram is not possible with known physics. We state exactly which discovery it needs and do not claim to have it.
  • Phase 0 is a visual effect from per-eye rendering, not a true hologram. We say so on stage. Phase 1 is true holography, limited by the window law.
  • Hand tracking from a single webcam is sensitive to lighting; mouse mode is the fallback for live demos.
  • Iris-based depth accuracy on real users has not been measured yet.
  • Literature figures quoted from memory are flagged in the theory document and will be checked.
  • Costs and specs in Phase 1 are estimates pending supplier quotes.
