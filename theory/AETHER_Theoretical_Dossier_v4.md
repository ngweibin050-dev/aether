# AETHER Theoretical Dossier: True Free-Space 3D Projection from First Principles

Oct 27, 2026 · @Ng Wei Bin

## Summary

The answer splits in two. A card-sized device can reconstruct a true 3D wavefront (correct focus, parallax, occlusion) under established physics, but only seen **through** its own aperture. A walk-around image floating in empty air, seen from directions that do not pass through the device, is incompatible with established physics unless ambient air has a light-emitting channel that known spectroscopy does not supply.

- **Tier W (window), feasible after engineering advances.** With eye tracking, a card needs only about 10^7 phase pixels at about 21–27 µm pitch. That is a phone-class pixel count, but it must be coherent and phase-modulating, which a phone OLED is not.
- **Tier F (free space), incompatible under stated assumptions.** In any linear, passive, matter-free medium, a point is visible only along rays that meet the device aperture (Theorem A). The only exits are matter at the point (excluded by project rule) or new light generated by the air at the point.
- **Strongest result (Theorem G, air-emission figure of merit).** For two-photon emission, a 113 lm human-size image needs an effective σ₂η of about 0.004–0.8 GM per air molecule, for 10–100 W average pump power, 10^11–10^12 W/cm² peak and 1 mm voxel depth. That number is modest. The obstruction is spectroscopic: N₂ and O₂ have no allowed two-photon transition reachable with eye-safe near-infrared light that emits visible light.
- **Most consequential open question.** Does any channel in ambient air (N₂, O₂, Ar, H₂O, CO₂) give visible emission with an effective nonlinear yield meeting Theorem G at eye-safe intensities?
- **Next derivation.** Generalise Theorem G to n = 3–4 photons with tabulated resonance data, which decides whether Tier F is closed or open.

All numbers come from `aether_dossier_v4.py` (in the project's aether/v4 folder). Labels used throughout: **Established** (textbook law), **Derived** (follows mathematically here), **Estimate** (depends on illustrative inputs), **Speculative** (unproven hypothesis). No literature search was run in this pass, so no novelty is claimed as established.

## Part I. Formal problem definition

The system is a planar device aperture Σ (area A, sides W × H) in otherwise empty air, which must make observers perceive a scene occupying a region V\_img in front of or around it.

**Symbols.** λ wavelength; k = 2π/λ; d viewer distance; D\_p pupil diameter; w viewing-window width; δ smallest resolved voxel; h height of an image point above Σ; L image luminance; f frame rate; τ motion-to-photon latency; N number of independently controlled optical modes.

**Illustrative targets (Estimate; chosen, not derived).**

| Parameter | Symbol | Value | Consequence if changed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aperture | W × H | 85.6 × 54 mm (bank card) | Cone angles scale with W/h |
| Wavelength | λ | 532 nm (green, worst-case visibility peak) | Pixel pitch scales with λ |
| Viewer distance | d | 0.4–0.5 m (hand-held); 1.5 m (room) | Mode count scales as 1/d² |
| Pupil | D\_p | 4 mm | Tracked mode count scales as D\_p² |
| Tracked window | w | 10 mm (pupil plus tracking margin) | Pitch p = λd/w |
| Voxel | δ | ≤ 0.3 mm at 0.5 m (≈ 2 arcmin) | Sets scan rate for emissive schemes |
| Luminance | L | 30 cd/m² (dim room) to 100 cd/m² | Power scales linearly |
| Human-size image area | S | 0.6 m² silhouette | Broadcast power scales with S |
| Frame rate, latency | f, τ | ≥ 60 Hz, ≤ 20 ms | Compute and SLM speed |

**Operational definition of success.** A scene point P is *displayed* to an eye at e when the field entering the pupil is, within diffraction, a spherical wave diverging from P. This gives correct direction (parallax), correct curvature (accommodation) and, summed over two eyes, correct vergence. Occlusion follows if the reconstructed field includes only the unoccluded parts of each object wave.

**Two tiers, defined before any solution.**

- **Tier W (window):** P is displayed to every eye position e for which the line e→P, extended, meets Σ.
- **Tier F (free space):** P is displayed to eye positions whose line e→P misses Σ, up to a full sphere of directions. This is the "walk around it, no device in the line of sight" requirement.

**Requirement list (from the brief) mapped to physics.** R1 wavefront reconstruction; R2 binocular and motion parallax; R3 accommodation; R4 occlusion, phase, amplitude, polarisation; R5 apparent volume; R6 multiple observers; R7 hand latency; R8 no headset; R9 smartphone role. Part III tests which of these can coexist.

## Part II. First-principles derivation

Everything below follows from Maxwell's equations in air plus one fact about the eye: it senses intensity across a pupil of about 4 mm. Quantum electrodynamics is not needed until Theorem G, where it enters only through measured transition strengths.

**II.1 From Maxwell to Helmholtz (Established).** In a linear, isotropic, homogeneous, source-free medium with time dependence e^{−iωt}, Maxwell's equations give the vector Helmholtz equation for each field component, with ∇·E = 0:

```latex
\nabla^2 \mathbf{E} + k^2 n^2 \mathbf{E} = 0, \qquad k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}
```

Air has n − 1 ≈ 2.9 × 10⁻⁴ and a Kerr coefficient n₂ of order 10⁻¹⁹ cm²/W, so at display intensities (≤ 1 W/cm²) the medium is linear to about one part in 10¹⁹. Assumption: no added matter, no gain.

**II.2 Scalar approximation (Established, validity stated).** One Cartesian component U obeys the same equation. Errors are of order (NA)² in polarisation-dependent terms. The eye is insensitive to polarisation, so the scalar model is adequate except at steep angles near the device and for glare from polarised optics.

**II.3 Angular spectrum (Established).** Any field radiated into z > 0 by the aperture plane is a superposition of plane waves:

```latex
U(x,y,z) = \iint \tilde{U}_0(k_x,k_y)\, e^{i(k_x x + k_y y + k_z z)}\, dk_x\, dk_y, \qquad k_z = \sqrt{k^2 - k_x^2 - k_y^2}
```

Components with k\_x² + k\_y² > k² are evanescent and decay within about λ. Consequence: a pixel pitch p can steer light only to sinθ ≤ λ/(2p). Structure finer than λ/2 carries no energy to the viewer.

**II.4 Kirchhoff–Helmholtz representation (Established).** For a source-free volume V with boundary ∂V, using the Green function G = e^{ik|r−r'|}/(4π|r−r'|):

```latex
U(\mathbf r) = \oint_{\partial V}\left[G\,\partial_{n'}U - U\,\partial_{n'}G\right] dS'
```

If the only non-zero boundary data are on Σ, every photon in the room was emitted by Σ. This is the starting point of Theorem A.

**II.5 Phase-space transport (Established for paraxial fields; Derived bound otherwise).** Define the Wigner distribution of the transverse field:

```latex
W(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{k}_t; z) = \int U^*\!\left(\mathbf{x}-\tfrac{\mathbf{s}}{2}\right) U\!\left(\mathbf{x}+\tfrac{\mathbf{s}}{2}\right) e^{-i\mathbf{k}_t\cdot\mathbf{s}}\, d^2 s, \qquad \partial_z W + \frac{\mathbf{k}_t}{k}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf x} W = 0
```

In the paraxial regime the transport equation is exact: W moves along straight lines without change. Non-paraxial corrections are of order λ/ℓ for structure of size ℓ. W can be negative only over regions of a few λ·(phase-space cell). Negative regions are what superoscillation exploits.

**II.6 Perception model (Established physiology, simplified).** The eye at e perceives a point at P when the pupil field has local wave vector pointing from P and wavefront curvature 1/|e − P|. Depth from focus needs curvature resolved across D\_p. Depth from vergence needs two pupils.

**II.7 Energy and radiance (Established).** Energy flows along the Poynting vector S = E × H and is conserved in a passive medium. Radiance (étendue) cannot increase through passive optics. Lasers have enormous radiance, so étendue limits efficiency of incoherent sources, not of laser systems.

**II.8 Degrees of freedom between two apertures (Established; Miller, Applied Optics 2000, from memory).** The number of well-coupled modes between a source area A\_T and a receiver area A\_R separated by L is

```latex
N \approx \frac{A_T\, A_R}{(\lambda L)^2}
```

This counts how many independent pieces of optical information the device can deliver into a pupil. It sets the minimum SLM pixel count for each architecture.

**II.9 The inverse problem (Derived).** Given a target field U\_T on the pupil set E (all eye positions to be served), find aperture data U\_0 on Σ such that the propagator P\_{Σ→E} U\_0 = U\_T. The propagator is compact with singular values that fall sharply after about N (II.8). So the problem has a solution only if U\_T lies, to the required accuracy, in the span of the first N singular vectors. For E inside the aperture cone this holds for physically reasonable scenes. For E outside the cone (Tier F) the needed singular values are exponentially small (Theorem A, superoscillation), so the solution does not exist in any energy-bounded sense.

## Part III. Fundamental feasibility analysis

Seven results decide the question. Theorems A–F are known physics applied with explicit assumptions; Theorem G is the new quantitative bound of this pass.

**Theorem A, visibility cone (Derived from II.4–II.5).** Assumptions: (A1) air is linear, (A2) passive, (A3) homogeneous outside the device, (A4) static over the propagation time, (A5) the pupil field has a non-negative Wigner distribution at the scale of the pupil (excludes superoscillation). Statement: an eye at e can perceive P only if the line from e through P, extended backwards, meets Σ, up to a diffraction blur of order λ/D\_p.

Proof sketch. By II.4, all light comes from Σ. By II.5, phase-space density travels on straight lines, so the direction content at the pupil is supported only on directions that point back to Σ. Perceiving P requires direction content pointing from P (II.6). Both conditions hold together only if the line e→P meets Σ. The result holds for real images in front of Σ and virtual images behind it.

For a point h above the card, the cone half-angle across the short side (54 mm) is 69.7° at h = 10 mm, 28.4° at 50 mm and 7.7° at 200 mm. A tall image floating high above a flat card is therefore seen only from nearly overhead.

**Theorem A′, cost of breaking A5 (Derived, Estimate).** Faking a direction outside the cone needs superoscillation across the pupil. Tilting by 0.3 rad over 4 mm needs about DΔθ/λ ≈ 2,260 extra oscillations. The energy fraction falls roughly as e^{−2260}. Allowed in principle, zero photons in practice.

**Theorem B, mode count (Derived from II.8).** Required independent modes:

| Configuration | Formula | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Card to one 4 mm pupil at 0.4 m | A·π(D\_p/2)²/(λd)² | 1.3 × 10⁶ |
| Card to a 10 mm tracked window at 0.4 m | A·w²/(λd)² | 1.0 × 10⁷ (pitch 21 µm) |
| Same at 0.5 m |  | 6.5 × 10⁶ (pitch 27 µm) |
| Untracked full parallax, ±30° | (W/p)(H/p), p = λ/(2 sin 30°) | 1.6 × 10¹⁰ (pitch 0.53 µm) |

The two rows for the tracked window agree exactly with the Fourier-hologram pitch formula p = λd/w, which is a consistency check. Eye tracking reduces the problem by a factor of about 1,600.

**Theorem C, smartphone optics (Derived).** A phone OLED with about 55 µm pitch diffracts green light to at most sinθ = λ/2p, that is ±0.28°. It is also incoherent and amplitude-only. It cannot synthesise a wavefront. What a phone does supply is compute (TFLOPS class GPU, Estimate), a front camera and depth sensor for eye and hand tracking, and an IMU. A coherent RGB source and a phase modulator at ≤ 27 µm pitch with about 10⁷ pixels are unavoidable additions for Tier W.

**Theorem D, power (Derived, Estimate).** Broadcasting a human-size image (L = 30 cd/m², S = 0.6 m², both faces) needs Φ = 2πLS ≈ 113 lm ≈ 0.17 W at 555 nm. Delivering a 10 × 10 cm image at 100 cd/m² into one 4 mm pupil at 0.4 m needs 7.9 × 10⁻⁵ lm ≈ 0.1 µW. Tracked delivery is about 10⁶ times more efficient.

**Theorem E, accommodation and viewers (Derived earlier; Theorems 3–5 of the Physics Record).** Inside its window, a hologram reproduces true wavefront curvature, so focus cues are correct. Serving V viewers needs about V × 2 × N\_pupil modes per frame. With a modulator of rate f\_SLM, viewers by time multiplexing ≤ f\_SLM/(2 × 60 Hz).

**Theorem F, linear media cannot localise light (Established).** For a beam of power P, the emission per unit length from any one-photon process is independent of focusing, so linear scattering lights the whole beam path. Only processes of order n ≥ 2 concentrate emission near a focus. This is why Tier F requires a nonlinear or active process in the air.

**Theorem G, air-emission figure of merit (Derived here).** Take a two-photon process with cross-section σ₂ and visible quantum yield η in air of number density n\_air = 2.5 × 10²⁵ m⁻³. For a focused Gaussian pump of power P(t), the integral of I² over all space is independent of focusing:

```latex
\iiint I^2\, dV = \int \frac{P^2}{\pi w(z)^2}\, dz = P^2\,\frac{z_R}{w_0^2} = \frac{\pi P^2}{\lambda_p}
```

Requiring the emitting length 2z\_R to equal the voxel depth δ\_z fixes w₀² = λ\_pδ\_z/(2π). Capping the peak intensity at I\_max fixes the peak power P\_pk = I\_maxλ\_pδ\_z/4. With average power P\_avg = P\_pk × duty, the visible photon rate is S = η n\_air σ₂ π I\_max δ\_z P\_avg/(4(hν)²). The required photon rate for 113 lm is S = 4.6 × 10¹⁷ s⁻¹, so

```latex
\sigma_2\,\eta \;\ge\; \frac{4\, S\, (h\nu)^2}{\pi\, n_{air}\, I_{max}\, \delta_z\, P_{avg}}
```

| Pump | P\_avg | I\_max (W/cm²) | δ\_z | Required σ₂η (GM) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1550 nm | 2 W (card thermal class) | 10¹¹ | 1 mm | 1.9 |
| 1550 nm | 10 W | 10¹¹ | 1 mm | 0.39 |
| 1550 nm | 100 W (mains dock) | 10¹¹ | 1 mm | 0.039 |
| 1550 nm | 100 W | 10¹² | 1 mm | 0.0039 |
| 1064 nm | 10 W | 10¹¹ | 1 mm | 0.82 |

(1 GM = 10⁻⁵⁰ cm⁴·s. I\_max = 10¹¹–10¹² W/cm² sits one to two orders below picosecond air breakdown, Estimate. The convention factor of 2 in the two-photon rate is absorbed into η.)

Two honest caveats. First, the two-photon emission profile along the beam is Lorentzian: half lies within ±z\_R and 94% within ±10 z\_R, so each voxel carries a dim axial halo. Second, the pump beam diverges after the focus and must be kept below eye-exposure limits wherever it travels; this was not computed here.

**Corollary G1, the obstruction is spectroscopic (Derived from Established spectroscopy, values from memory).** A requirement of 0.004–2 GM is modest; ordinary fluorescent dyes reach 1–100 GM. But a two-photon process with 0.8–1.2 eV pump photons reaches only 1.6–2.3 eV. In that range N₂ and Ar have no states at all; their lowest excited states lie above about 6 eV. O₂ has two low singlets, a¹Δg (0.98 eV) and b¹Σg⁺ (1.63 eV, emitting at 762 nm), but both are spin-forbidden from the ground state. Their emission is also nearly invisible: at 762 nm the eye's sensitivity is about 6 × 10⁻⁵ of its peak, so 113 lm would need about 2.8 kW of 762 nm light. So no two-photon, eye-safe, visible channel exists in ambient air. Higher orders (n ≥ 4 to reach 6 eV with near-infrared light) lead to multiphoton ionisation and plasma at about 10¹³–10¹⁴ W/cm².

## Part IV. Architecture search

Eight architectures were tested against Theorems A–G. Only one reaches true wavefront reconstruction with today's physics, and it is Tier W. No architecture reaches Tier F without matter or plasma.

| # | Architecture | Mechanism | Tier | Status | Deciding constraint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Eye-tracked holographic window | Coherent RGB + phase SLM (\~10⁷ px, 21–27 µm) + field lens + window steering; phone does tracking and CGH compute | W | Feasible after engineering advances | A, B; SLM size, steering, laser safety |
| A2 | Untracked full-parallax CGH | Sub-micron phase pixels over the whole card | W | Feasible in principle; prohibitive | B: 1.6 × 10¹⁰ px |
| A3 | Phone screen only | Head-tracked rendering on OLED | None (2D with motion parallax) | Works today; not holography | C |
| A4 | Room walls as hologram plate | Wavefront shaping through diffuse walls | F (geometry) | Closed by energy | H3: η ≤ N/N\_tot ≈ 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁵ |
| A5 | Superoscillating pupil field | Negative-Wigner direction faking | F | Closed | A′: e^{−2260} |
| A6 | Matter at P (trapped particle) | Acoustic or photophoretic trap, scanned and lit | F | Works physically; excluded by project rule; not a wavefront | Founder rule: nothing released into air |
| A7 | Air plasma voxels | Multiphoton ionisation at focus | F | Known; unsafe, noisy | \~10¹³–10¹⁴ W/cm² |
| A8 | Low-threshold air emission | Nonlinear visible emission from ambient gas at eye-safe intensity | F | Requires a channel not in known spectroscopy | G, G1 |

**A1 in detail (the only Tier W path).** Geometry: SLM on the card, viewer at d. A field lens of focal length about d images the light source onto the viewer's pupil, forming a viewing window of width w = λd/p. The camera tracks each pupil and the window is steered to it each frame. Equations: the CGH is a Fresnel or angular-spectrum propagation of the scene to Σ (II.3), cost about N log₂N per depth layer per colour. With N = 10⁷, that is about 2.3 × 10⁸ operations per layer, or about 2.5 × 10¹² per second for 30 layers × 3 colours × 2 eyes × 60 Hz (Estimate; complex arithmetic adds a factor of a few). That is at the edge of a current phone GPU. Prior art: SeeReal's tracked-viewing-window holography (from memory, not re-verified). What is new for AETHER is only the card form factor, so no novelty is claimed.

**A1 open engineering items.** (1) Steering range: the window must follow the eye over the whole cone without mechanical parts. (2) A thin field lens over 85 × 54 mm with low aberration. (3) Laser eye safety at the pupil, which Theorem D makes easy (microwatts). (4) Speckle: coherent illumination causes speckle; time-averaging over sub-frames or partial coherence reduces it at the cost of SLM rate.

**A6 and A7, why they still matter.** They prove that Tier F is not forbidden by physics in general. It is forbidden only once air is required to stay unmodified (A6) and intensities eye-safe (A7). Published examples, from memory: photophoretic optical-trap display (Smalley et al., Nature 2018); acoustic levitation display (Hirayama et al., Nature 2019); femtosecond air-plasma voxels (Ochiai et al., 2016).

**Physically coupled variables.** Pitch, window size and distance are tied by p = λd/w; you cannot enlarge the window without shrinking pixels. Float height and viewing cone are tied by Theorem A; a higher image is seen from a narrower cone. Peak intensity, voxel depth and pump power are tied by Theorem G. Independent variables: wavelength choice (within the visible), frame rate, number of tracked viewers (bounded by SLM rate), and compute.

**Limiting-case checks.** As w → D\_p, the tracked mode count tends to the Miller pupil count, as it must. As h → 0, the cone opens to 90°, recovering an ordinary flat display. As δ\_z → ∞ in Theorem G, the requirement falls to zero, which is correct: an unlocalised glow along the whole beam needs no focusing, but it is not a voxel. ## Part VI. Strongest candidate contribution

The strongest candidate is Theorem G with Corollary G1: it turns "can air glow at a chosen point?" into one measurable number, σ₂η, with a threshold of 0.004–2 GM.

**Why it is more than a renamed known result.** The ingredients are textbook: the focus-independence of total two-photon yield is standard in multiphoton microscopy. What is not standard, as far as this pass can tell, is the following. First, the threshold is derived for free-space display, combining the voxel-depth constraint, a peak-intensity cap and the photon rate of a target image. Second, it shows the energy budget is not what blocks Tier F. Third, it locates the block exactly: the absence of an allowed, visible-emitting two-photon channel in N₂, O₂ and Ar for eye-safe pump photons.

**What it changes.** Before this result, the open problem was stated as "lower the intensity 10⁴–10⁶×", which is vague. After it, the problem is a search over a finite list of gas-phase transitions for one property. Any candidate channel can be accepted or rejected by looking up or measuring σ₂ and η.

**Second contribution (weaker).** Theorem B reconciles three counts that are usually quoted separately: the Miller mode count to a pupil, the Fourier-hologram pitch p = λd/w, and the phone panel resolution. It shows a tracked card hologram needs about as many pixels as a phone already has (10⁷), so the gap is pixel physics (phase, coherence), not pixel count.

**Novelty status.** Not established. No literature search was run in this pass. Both results could exist in the volumetric-display or multiphoton literature.

| Walk-around image if an ambient-air channel with σ₂η ≳ 0.004–2 GM (or an n-photon equivalent) exists | **Feasible only if that assumption is satisfied; currently underdetermined** for n ≥ 3 | Theorem G; open question 1 |

**Strongest mathematical result.** Theorem G: the visible photon rate of two-photon air emission is S = η n\_air σ₂ π I\_max δ\_z P\_avg/(4(hν)²), independent of focusing once voxel depth is fixed. Hence Tier F needs σ₂η ≥ 0.004–2 GM per air molecule, a modest value that ambient air nevertheless does not supply at eye-safe pump wavelengths.

**Single most consequential unanswered question.** Does any channel in ambient air, at any multiphoton order, produce visible light with an effective yield that satisfies the n-photon form of Theorem G below eye-safety and breakdown limits? If no, Tier F is closed under established physics and AETHER is a Tier W device. If yes, it is the core invention.

**Next derivation that most reduces uncertainty.** Generalise Theorem G to order n: compute ∭ Iⁿ dV for a Gaussian focus (it now depends on w₀), add the voxel-depth and intensity caps, and solve for the required σ\_nη. Then insert tabulated three- and four-photon resonance data for N₂, O₂, H₂O and CO₂. This is pure calculation using published data, needs no laboratory, and returns a yes or no for each channel.
